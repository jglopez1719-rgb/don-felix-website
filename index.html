Keep it good at home

Warming & cooking help

Bought it cooked? Keep it covered and use a thermometer. Bought it raw? These are the basic safe temperatures to know.

Reheating our BBQ

These are general instructions for food that was chilled safely. If we give you different instructions with a special order, follow those.

Ribs

Heat the oven to 325°F. Put the ribs meat-side up in an oven-safe dish, add a small splash of water or broth, and cover tightly with foil. Heat until the center reaches 165°F. Uncover for the last few minutes only if you want the outside drier.

Tri-tip or brisket

Heat the oven to 325°F. Keep any juices with the meat. If it is sliced, overlap the slices in a covered dish so the edges do not dry out. Add a little broth or water if needed and heat to 165°F.

Chicken

Place the chicken in a covered oven-safe dish with a small splash of water or broth. Reheat at 325°F and check the thickest part with a food thermometer. It should reach 165°F.

Mac & cheese

Put it in a covered oven-safe dish, stir in a small splash of milk or water, and heat at 325°F. Stir once while warming and heat throughout to 165°F.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, use refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days, and reheat to 165°F. See USDA leftovers guidance.

Cooking raw meat

Use a food thermometer in the thickest part. Color alone is not a reliable safety check.

What you’re cookingMinimum internal temperature
Beef, pork or lamb steaks, chops and roasts145°F + 3-minute rest
Ground meat and sausage160°F
Chicken and turkey165°F
Previously cooked leftovers165°F
These are general federal food-safety minimums, not a recipe. See the complete FoodSafety.gov temperature chart.
